# Symmetry Guide

Goal
Evoke a sense of perfection, authority, manufactured beauty, or surrealism.

Theory
Perfect symmetry is rare in nature. When an image is perfectly mirrored across the vertical or horizontal axis, it feels highly designed, intentional, and sometimes unnerving. It commands attention.

Application in Filmmaking
- **The "Wes Anderson" Look:** Quirky, flat, highly stylized center-framing.
- **Luxury and Power:** A CEO sitting dead center at a long table. It screams dominance and control.
- **Reflections:** Using water or glass to create horizontal symmetry (often used in automotive or sci-fi).

AI Prompting Tokens
- "perfectly symmetrical composition"
- "centered subject, mirror reflection"
- "geometric symmetry, Wes Anderson style framing"

When NOT to Use Symmetry
- When the scene is meant to feel chaotic, documentary-style, natural, or relaxed. Symmetry requires strict blocking and set design, so it always feels "staged."
