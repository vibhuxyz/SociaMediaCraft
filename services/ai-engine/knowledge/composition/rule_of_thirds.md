# Rule of Thirds Guide

Goal
Create balanced, naturally pleasing compositions that guide the viewer's eye without feeling rigid or artificial.

Theory
Imagine a 3x3 grid over the image. The most visually compelling areas are the four intersections where the lines meet. Placing subjects or horizons along these lines creates more tension, energy, and interest than simply centering the subject.

Application in Filmmaking
- **Eyes:** In a medium shot or close-up, place the character's eyes on the top-third horizontal line.
- **Horizons:** Place the horizon line on the top third (to emphasize the foreground/ground) or the bottom third (to emphasize the sky/environment).
- **Interviews/Dialogue:** Place the speaking character on the left or right vertical third, leaving the other two-thirds as "lead room" (the direction they are looking).

AI Prompting Tokens
- "composed using rule of thirds"
- "subject positioned on the left third"
- "off-center composition"

When to Break the Rule
- When you want to convey ultimate power, authority, or artificial perfection (use Symmetry/Center framing instead).
- When creating extreme tension by deliberately cramming a subject into the corner of the frame.
