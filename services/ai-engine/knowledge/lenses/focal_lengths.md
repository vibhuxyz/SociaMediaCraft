# Focal Lengths Guide

Goal
Choose the correct focal length to manipulate perspective, compression, and the emotional distance between the audience and the subject.

Key Focal Lengths

1. **Ultra-Wide (12mm - 18mm):**
   - *Effect:* Exaggerates distance. Objects close to the lens look massive; objects far away look tiny. Creates a distorted, intense, or highly stylized look.
   - *Usage:* Action sports, comedic distortion, or making small rooms look huge (real estate).

2. **Wide (24mm - 35mm):**
   - *Effect:* The storytelling lens. Gives excellent environmental context while keeping the subject grounded. (35mm is considered the classic movie focal length).
   - *Usage:* Establishing shots, documentary-style follow shots, group scenes.

3. **Standard/Normal (50mm):**
   - *Effect:* Closely mimics human vision. Minimal distortion. Feels objective and natural.
   - *Usage:* Medium shots, standard narrative dialogue, honest/authentic branding.

4. **Telephoto (85mm - 135mm):**
   - *Effect:* Compresses the background, making distant objects appear closer to the subject. Highly flattering for human faces (slims the face).
   - *Usage:* Professional portraits, beauty/fashion ads, isolating a subject in a crowd.

5. **Super Telephoto (200mm+):**
   - *Effect:* Extreme background compression. Can make a subject look completely flat against the background (e.g., a massive sunset right behind them).
   - *Usage:* Sports, wildlife, or hyper-stylized fashion.
