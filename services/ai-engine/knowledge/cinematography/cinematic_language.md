# Cinematic Language Guide

Goal
Understand the overarching grammar of film to ensure scenes cut together logically and emotionally.

The Basics of Film Grammar

1. **The 180-Degree Rule:**
   - An imaginary line connects two interacting subjects. The camera must stay on one side of this line to maintain consistent screen direction. If Character A is on the left looking right, and Character B is on the right looking left, crossing the line will suddenly make them look like they are facing the same direction.

2. **Screen Direction:**
   - If a car drives from Left to Right in shot 1, it must continue driving Left to Right in shot 2, unless we see it turn.

3. **Kuleshov Effect:**
   - The juxtaposition of two shots creates a third meaning. 
   - Shot A (Man looking) + Shot B (Soup) = Hunger. 
   - Shot A (Man looking) + Shot B (Crying child) = Pity.

4. **Visual Rhythm:**
   - The pacing of the shots. Fast cuts = high energy/chaos. Slow, long takes = calm/tension.

Application for the Story Architect & Shot Planner
When writing the Shot Plan, ensure that the sequence of shots tells a visual story even if the sound is muted. Every cut should be motivated by an action, a look, or a new piece of information.
