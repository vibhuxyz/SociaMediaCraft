# Shot Types (Scale) Guide

Goal
Define the size of the subject within the frame to control intimacy and environmental context.

Key Shot Sizes

1. **Extreme Wide Shot (EWS) / Establishing Shot:**
   - *Usage:* Shows the environment. The character is a speck. Sets the time, place, and scale.
   - *Prompt:* "Extreme wide shot," "sweeping landscape," "establishing shot of the city."

2. **Wide Shot (WS) / Full Shot:**
   - *Usage:* Shows the character from head to toe. Balances the character with their environment. Good for action or fashion.
   - *Prompt:* "Full body shot," "wide shot."

3. **Medium Shot (MS):**
   - *Usage:* From the waist up. The standard workhorse of filmmaking. Good for dialogue and body language.
   - *Prompt:* "Medium shot," "waist up."

4. **Medium Close-Up (MCU):**
   - *Usage:* Chest to top of head. Increases intimacy while still allowing for some shoulder/hand gestures.
   - *Prompt:* "Medium close up," "chest up portrait."

5. **Close-Up (CU):**
   - *Usage:* Shows just the head and shoulders (or a specific object). Pure emotion. Eliminates the background context.
   - *Prompt:* "Close up of face," "tight portrait."

6. **Extreme Close-Up (ECU) / Macro:**
   - *Usage:* Focuses on a specific detail (an eye, a watch dial, condensation on a glass). Intense, abstract, sensory.
   - *Prompt:* "Extreme close up," "macro photography," "detail shot of."

Rule of Thumb for the Shot Planner
Do not string together three of the same shot types in a row (e.g., CU -> CU -> CU) unless building extreme tension. Vary the scale (WS -> MCU -> ECU) to keep the edit dynamic.
