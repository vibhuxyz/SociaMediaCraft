# Framing Guide

Goal
Control where the subject sits within the boundaries of the image to create balance, tension, or focus.

Key Concepts

1. **Rule of Thirds:**
   - *Definition:* Dividing the frame into a 3x3 grid and placing the subject on the intersecting lines.
   - *Usage:* Creates a natural, aesthetically pleasing composition.
   - *Prompt:* "Composed using rule of thirds, subject on the left third."

2. **Center Framing / Symmetry:**
   - *Definition:* Placing the subject dead-center.
   - *Usage:* Creates authority, artificial perfection, or an authoritarian/Wes Anderson vibe. Very popular in modern luxury ads.
   - *Prompt:* "Perfectly centered composition," "symmetrical framing."

3. **Short Siding vs. Lead Room:**
   - *Lead Room:* Framing a character on the left side of the screen while they look toward the right. Creates a comfortable space for their gaze.
   - *Short Siding:* Framing a character on the left side of the screen while they look left (into the edge of the frame). Creates claustrophobia or unease.

4. **Negative Space:**
   - *Definition:* Leaving a large portion of the frame empty.
   - *Usage:* Isolates the character, implies loneliness, or leaves room for graphic overlays (logos, text).
   - *Prompt:* "Vast negative space," "minimalist composition."

5. **Frames within Frames:**
   - *Definition:* Using environmental elements (doorways, windows, tree branches) to create a secondary frame around the subject.
   - *Usage:* Adds depth and a sense of voyeurism.
   - *Prompt:* "Framed through a doorway," "looking through a window."
