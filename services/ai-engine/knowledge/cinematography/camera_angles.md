# Camera Angles Guide

Goal
Use the height and tilt of the camera relative to the subject to establish psychological power dynamics.

Key Angles

1. **Eye-Level:**
   - *Psychology:* Neutral, empathetic. We see the character as an equal.
   - *Usage:* Standard dialogue scenes, relatable commercial casting.
   - *Prompt:* "Eye-level angle."

2. **Low Angle:**
   - *Psychology:* The camera looks UP at the subject. Makes the subject look powerful, intimidating, heroic, or dominant.
   - *Usage:* Hero shots, luxury brand reveals, sports commercials.
   - *Prompt:* "Low angle shot, looking up at the subject."

3. **High Angle:**
   - *Psychology:* The camera looks DOWN at the subject. Makes the subject look vulnerable, small, or insignificant.
   - *Usage:* Showing a character overwhelmed by their environment, emotional low points.
   - *Prompt:* "High angle shot, looking down at the subject."

4. **Overhead / Top-Down / God's Eye:**
   - *Psychology:* Omniscient, graphic, detached. Great for showing patterns, symmetry, or a character's exact position in a maze/environment.
   - *Usage:* Food prep videos (overhead table), architectural showcases.
   - *Prompt:* "Top-down overhead shot," "God's eye view," "flat lay perspective."

5. **Dutch Angle (Canted):**
   - *Psychology:* Uneasy, disorienting, psychological tension. The horizon line is tilted.
   - *Usage:* Horror, intense thriller moments, high-fashion editorial (for edge).
   - *Prompt:* "Dutch angle, tilted horizon."

AI Prompting Note
Always specify the angle in relation to the subject. If you just say "low angle," the AI might generate a low-resolution image. Say: "Camera placed low to the ground pointing up at the car."
