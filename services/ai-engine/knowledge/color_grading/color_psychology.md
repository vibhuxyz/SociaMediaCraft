# Color Psychology Guide

Goal
Help the Art Director and Prompt Engineer choose the dominant color palette based on the emotional response required from the audience.

The Psychology of Key Colors

1. **Red:**
   - *Emotions:* Passion, urgency, danger, excitement, appetite.
   - *Use in Ads:* Fast food, clearance sales, sports cars, romantic luxury.

2. **Blue:**
   - *Emotions:* Trust, calm, intelligence, corporate stability, coldness.
   - *Use in Ads:* Tech companies, banks, healthcare, refreshing beverages.

3. **Green:**
   - *Emotions:* Nature, health, wealth, growth, tranquility.
   - *Use in Ads:* Organic food, financial planning, outdoor gear, wellness.

4. **Yellow:**
   - *Emotions:* Happiness, optimism, youth, caution.
   - *Use in Ads:* Budget airlines, summer fashion, energetic youth brands.

5. **Purple:**
   - *Emotions:* Royalty, mystery, spirituality, creativity.
   - *Use in Ads:* High-end chocolate, beauty products, fantasy entertainment.

6. **Orange:**
   - *Emotions:* Energy, fun, affordability, warmth.
   - *Use in Ads:* Fitness, children's toys, friendly tech.

Application in the AI Workflow
- **Monochromatic:** Using variations of a single color to create a highly stylized, focused emotional impact.
- **Analogous:** Using colors next to each other on the wheel (e.g., Blue and Green) for a harmonious, natural look.
- **Complementary:** Using opposite colors (e.g., Red and Green) to create high tension and visual pop.

Checklist
- [ ] Does the dominant color of the scene match the emotion of the script?
- [ ] Are background colors distracting from the brand's primary color?
